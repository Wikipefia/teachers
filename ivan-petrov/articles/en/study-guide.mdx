# Math Exam Study Guide

This guide covers everything you need to prepare for the Linear Algebra and Discrete Mathematics exams. I've structured it based on the most common mistakes I see year after year.

<Callout type="warning">
This guide supplements — but does not replace — working through the problem sets. There are no shortcuts in mathematics.
</Callout>

## Exam Format

<Tabs>
<Tab label="Linear Algebra">

## Topic Checklist

Use this checklist to track your preparation:

### Linear Algebra Core Topics

1. **Vector Spaces**: Can you check if a set is a subspace? (3 conditions)
2. **Linear Independence**: Can you determine if vectors are independent?
3. **Basis & Dimension**: Can you find a basis for a given subspace?
4. **Matrix Operations**: Multiplication, transpose, inverse — compute by hand
5. **Determinants**: Cofactor expansion, row operations, properties
6. **Eigenvalues**: Characteristic polynomial → eigenvalues → eigenvectors
7. **Diagonalization**: When is it possible? How to construct $P$ and $D$?

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Callout type="error">
**#1 mistake every year**: Forgetting that matrix multiplication is NOT commutative. $AB \neq BA$ in general. Always check the order.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error">
**#2 mistake**: Computing determinants incorrectly due to sign errors in cofactor expansion. Use the checkerboard sign pattern:
$$\begin{pmatrix} + & - & + \\ - & + & - \\ + & - & + \end{pmatrix}$$
</Callout>

## Practice Problem: Diagonalization

<MathBlock>
\text{Diagonalize } A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
</MathBlock>

<Collapse title="Step-by-step solution">

**Step 1**: Find eigenvalues via $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$\det \begin{pmatrix} 4-\lambda & 1 \\ 2 & 3-\lambda \end{pmatrix} = (4-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0$$

$$(\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 2) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 5, \quad \lambda_2 = 2$$

**Step 2**: Find eigenvectors for each $\lambda$:

For $\lambda_1 = 5$: $(A - 5I)\mathbf{v} = 0$
$$\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v} = 0 \implies \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

For $\lambda_2 = 2$: $(A - 2I)\mathbf{v} = 0$
$$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v} = 0 \implies \mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Step 3**: Construct $P$ and $D$:

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}, \quad D = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Verification**: $A = PDP^{-1}$ ✓
</Collapse>

## Verify With Code

<CodePlayground language="python">
import numpy as np

A = np.array([[4, 1], [2, 3]])

# Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors
eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eig(A)
print(f"Eigenvalues: {eigenvalues}")
print(f"Eigenvectors:\n{eigenvectors}")

# Verify diagonalization: A = P D P^(-1)
P = eigenvectors
D = np.diag(eigenvalues)
A_reconstructed = P @ D @ np.linalg.inv(P)
print(f"\nReconstructed A:\n{np.round(A_reconstructed, 2)}")
print(f"Match: {np.allclose(A, A_reconstructed)}")
</CodePlayground>

## Final Tips

<Callout type="info">
**The night before the exam**: Don't try to learn new material. Review your cheat sheet, do 2–3 easy problems to build confidence, then get a full night's sleep. A rested brain solves more problems than an exhausted one that crammed until 3 AM.
</Callout>
